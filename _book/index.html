1 Introduction

This book is intended for journalists, political professionals, and students who hope to become journalists or political professionals to learn the principles and practices of data analysis. It is based on course materials developed at George Washington University’s School of Media and Public Affairs.

  • Political Polling (generated from slides by Gemini)
    • Historical Context of Polling Misses: The presentation introduces the topic with notable historical polling failures, including the 1936 Literary Digest poll and the inaccurate “Dewey Defeats Truman” headline from 1948, to establish the long-standing challenge of polling accuracy.
    • The 2016 Election and Non-Response Bias: This section analyzes the 2016 presidential election, arguing that polling errors were primarily due to a “missing at random” (MAR) non-response bias, where pollsters failed to adequately weight responses by voters’ education levels.
    • The 2020 Election and Compounding Biases: The 2020 election presented further challenges, including the COVID-19 pandemic and record turnout. The errors in these polls are attributed to a combination of MAR non-response bias and “missing not at random” (MNAR) bias, which includes the “shy Trump voter” theory.
    • The 2022 Midterms and Partisan Polling: For the 2022 elections, the focus shifts to the growing influence of partisan-aligned pollsters, whose over-representation in polling averages created a misleading expectation of a “red wave,” and the phenomenon of “herding,” where pollsters adjust results to match consensus.
    • The 2024 Election and New Weighting Techniques: Analysis of the 2024 election shows that pollsters attempted to correct for previous errors by introducing new techniques, such as weighting results based on respondents’ “recalled vote” from past elections, though challenges with “likely voter” models remained.
    • Optional module: NEW AI and political polling
    • Understanding Types of Polling Error: The presentation provides a theoretical foundation by defining key types of non-sampling errors, including noncoverage, nonresponse, and measurement error, and explains the statistical concepts of Missing Completely at Random (MCAR), Missing at Random (MAR), and Missing Not at Random (MNAR).
    • Modern Survey Sampling Methodologies: This section outlines the various methods pollsters use to gather data, such as list-based sampling from voter files, address-based sampling (ABS), random-digit dialing (RDD), and the increasingly common use of non-probability online opt-in panels.
    • Principles of Effective Survey Design: The final part offers practical guidance on writing good survey questions to avoid “satisficing,” where respondents give low-effort answers. Key recommendations include keeping surveys brief, using simple and unambiguous language, and avoiding double-barreled or leading questions.
    • Application: return to the EDA of a poll conducted earlier and see how proper weighting can change the results
